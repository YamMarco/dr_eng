| TEACHER OVERVIEW — L2

LESSON 2  —  Eye Catchers 1: Numbers and Names
P2: Number in question → find it in text → match the source
P3: Name in question → go to their paragraph → read only there
Objective
By the end of this lesson the student uses numbers and proper names in questions as navigation pointers — going directly to the relevant paragraph instead of reading everything.
Key insight to convey
"A number in the question is a GPS coordinate. It tells you exactly where to go. A name is the same: it is always attached to a specific paragraph. Find the name → you found the paragraph."
Timing plan

Common mistakes to watch for
· Student sees "85%" in the question but scans the whole text for it instead of going straight to paragraph III
· Student sees "Dr. Santos" and goes to paragraph II — correct, but then reads the whole paragraph instead of scanning for the specific number
· Student confuses two numbers (e.g., 85% and 40%) because they do not identify which noun the number is attached to
· Student skips P1 and just reads randomly — redirect: "Step 1 is still the question. Step 2 is still keywords. The Eye Catcher IS the keyword today." |

| THE CITY GARDEN PROJECT
I     Five years ago, the streets of Greenville had almost no plants or trees. The air was polluted, and most residents felt that the city was an unpleasant place to live. A local charity decided to change this. They planted over 2,000 trees and created 15 community gardens across the city. Today, Greenville looks very different.
II     The person behind this change is Dr. Maria Santos, a professor at Greenville University. "We wanted to show that any city can become greener in less than ten years," she explains. Her team worked with more than 300 volunteers from the local community. The volunteers spent an average of three hours a week working in the gardens.
III     The results were surprising. According to the charity, 85% of Greenville residents now say the city is a better place to live. Dr. Santos's colleague, Professor James Wu, found that stress levels among residents had fallen by 40%. "When people have access to green spaces, they feel calmer and happier," he says. The project now receives visits from city planners from over 50 countries who want to learn from Greenville's success. |
| --- |
| TEACHER
After reading — class discussion (2 min):
Ask: "How many numbers did you find?" → 8 (Five years, 2,000, 15, ten years, 300, three hours, 85%, 40%, 50).
Ask: "How many names?" → 3 (Dr. Maria Santos, Greenville University, Professor James Wu).
Ask: "Which paragraph has Dr. Santos in it?" → II. "Which has Professor Wu?" → III.
"Names are paragraph pointers. The question mentions a name → you know exactly where to go." |

| TEACHER — I DO SCRIPT
I DO — SAY ALOUD for Q1 (P2 — number question):
Q1: "How many trees did the charity plant?" (paragraph I)
STEP 1 — QUESTION: "How many trees did the charity plant?"
I am NOT reading the text yet.
STEP 2 — KEYWORD: The question says "how many trees" → I am looking for a NUMBER attached to "trees".
P2: I scan for a number near the word "trees" — NOT for any number.
STEP 3 — LOCATE: In paragraph I I see: "planted over 2,000 trees". The number is 2,000.
Point to "2,000 trees" in paragraph I. Show it is in the right paragraph.
STEP 4 — ANSWER: "over 2,000 trees".

I DO — SAY ALOUD for Q2 (P3 — name question):
Q2: "According to Dr. Maria Santos, what did her team want to show?"
STEP 1 — QUESTION: "According to Dr. Maria Santos, what did her team want to show?"
I see a NAME. P3 activates.
STEP 2 — P3: Name in question = "Dr. Maria Santos" → go to her paragraph.
Ask students: "Which paragraph has Dr. Santos?" → Paragraph II.
STEP 3 — READ ONLY PARAGRAPH II: "We wanted to show that any city can become greener in less than ten years."
Underline that sentence. Do NOT read paragraph I or III.
STEP 4 — ANSWER: "that any city can become greener in less than ten years."

After modelling both: "P2 = use the number to navigate. P3 = use the name to navigate. Both save time." |

| 4.  What do we learn from Professor James Wu about the results of the project? (paragraph III)
Circle the number of the correct answer. |
| --- |
| ANSWER KEY
Answer: ii) — How stress levels changed among residents
P3 applied: "Professor James Wu" → paragraph III → "stress levels among residents had fallen by 40%."
Distractor analysis:
i) Not mentioned — no reason for his study is given.
iii) True information (50 countries) but that is about the project overall, not what Wu found.
iv) No disagreement mentioned in the text.
Key teaching point: option iii) uses a real number from paragraph III — classic distractor. The name "Wu" tells you to look for what HE found specifically. |
| --- |
| TEACHER
Class debrief after YOU DO (3 min):
Ask: "Which Eye Catcher led you to paragraph III?" → "Professor James Wu" (P3).
Ask: "Who chose iii)? What was tempting about it?" → 50 countries is real but not from Wu.
"A distractor uses true information in the wrong context. P3 protects you: Wu → his paragraph → his finding." |

| SPORT FOR ALL
A new community sports programme in the city of Portland has helped over 4,000 residents become more active over the past two years. The programme offers free fitness classes three times a week in 12 different neighbourhoods. According to Dr. Laura Kim, who runs the programme, the idea is simple. "We bring the sport to the people, not the people to the sport," she says. "Most people want to be active — they just need the opportunity." Professor Marcus Allen of Portland State University found that participants in the programme were 60% less likely to suffer from stress-related health problems. |
