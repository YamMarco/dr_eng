| TEACHER OVERVIEW — L10

LESSON 10  —  Inference Questions — Reading Between the Lines
P13: Implied → Combine two pieces → Not stated directly → From the text only

Objective
By the end of this lesson the student reaches a conclusion the text implies but does not state, using only information from the text.
Key insight
"An inference question has no single sentence that is the answer. The answer is built by combining two pieces of information from the text. Students who bring outside knowledge will almost always choose the wrong option. The inference must come FROM THE TEXT — not from what makes logical sense to the student."
Timing plan

Common mistakes to watch for
· Student brings in outside knowledge — "but I know from real life that..."  → "Only from the text"
· Student chooses the option that sounds most logical — not the one implied by the text
· Student cannot identify what two pieces to combine — teach them to find both before committing
· Student confuses inference with main idea — inference requires combining; main idea is in one sentence |

| THE EXERCISE MYTH
I     Most people believe that to get fit, they need to exercise for at least 30 minutes every day. However, new research suggests this idea may be wrong. According to a study published in the Journal of Sports Science, short bursts of intense exercise — as little as seven minutes a day — can be just as effective as longer workouts. This finding challenges everything most fitness experts have said for decades.
II     Dr. Jennifer Cho, a sports scientist at Yonsei University in South Korea, tested two groups of volunteers for 12 weeks. The first group exercised for 30 minutes every day. The second group did seven minutes of intense exercise three times a day. At the end, Dr. Cho found that both groups had similar improvements. However, the second group reported feeling more energetic and less stressed.
III     Not all scientists agree with these findings. Professor Robert Walsh of Oxford University argues that the research has serious limitations. "The study only looked at healthy young adults," he says. "We cannot apply these results to older people or those with health problems." He also points out that most people cannot maintain these routines for more than a few weeks. |
| --- |
| TEACHER — I DO
I DO — Q1 (inference):
Q1: "What can we infer about the seven-minute exercise routine from paragraph II?"
P8: "What can we infer?" = inference → P13 activates.
Piece 1: "the second group did seven minutes three times a day" — they exercised less total time.
Piece 2: "both groups had similar improvements" — same results.
Combine: a shorter exercise routine can produce the same results as a longer one.
This is NOT stated — it is implied. Answer: iii) — less time can be as effective. |
| --- |
| 1.  What can we infer about the seven-minute exercise routine from the results in paragraph II?
Circle the correct answer. |
| --- |
| ANSWER KEY
Answer: iii) — A shorter routine can produce similar results to a longer one.
P13: Piece 1 (second group: 7 min × 3) + Piece 2 (both groups had similar improvements) → shorter = same results.
i) is in text (paragraph III says "only healthy young adults") — but it is stated, not inferred. The inference must go BEYOND what is stated. |

| THE MEMORY STUDY
Scientists at Cambridge University have discovered that people remember information better when they write it by hand than when they type it. In a study of 150 university students, those who took notes by hand scored 25% higher in memory tests than those who typed. The researchers explain that handwriting requires people to process and rephrase the information, rather than simply copying it. However, not all the students who handwrote scored higher. Those who wrote very slowly and could not keep up with the information performed no better than those who typed. |
| --- |
| T1.  What can we infer from the text about why handwriting improves memory?
Circle the correct answer. |
| --- |
| T2.  What does "it" refer to in the sentence "It requires people to process and rephrase the information"?
Give ONE answer. |
| --- |
| ANSWER KEY
T1: ii) — The brain works harder when processing information by hand.
P13: "requires people to process and rephrase" → implies more brain effort → better memory.
T2: handwriting.
P7: "it" → sentence before → "handwriting" → substitute: "Handwriting requires people to process..." ✓ |
