LESSON 23  —  Full Simulation Exam
90 minutes · All patterns · Exam conditions · No help

Objective
By the end of this lesson the student completes a full Module C exam under exam conditions, using all 15 patterns automatically.
Key insight
"This is the closest you will get to the real exam before the real exam. Every pattern you have learned, every vocabulary word, every strategy — all of it is being tested today. The only way to fail this lesson is to not use the patterns."
Timing plan

Common mistakes to watch for
· Student does not use P15 and freezes on a hard question
· Student writes 55 words and thinks that is enough
· Student runs out of time for writing — remind: writing starts at minute 40
· Student does not check subject+verb in every sentence of the writing task |

| THE KINDNESS EFFECT
I     Most people believe that being kind to others is good for those who receive the kindness. However, new research suggests that the person who gives is often the greatest beneficiary. According to a study conducted at three universities across the United States, people who regularly perform acts of kindness report feeling significantly happier and less anxious. Over 5,000 participants took part in the study, which lasted for two years.
II     Dr. Grace Chen, a psychologist at the University of California, found that even small acts of kindness can have a lasting positive effect. "The brain actually releases chemicals when we help others," explains Dr. Chen. "These chemicals are similar to those released during exercise." She adds that the effect is strongest when people perform acts of kindness for people they do not know.
III     Professor Tom Adeyemi of King's College London argues that schools should teach kindness as a formal subject. "Kindness may be the most important skill of all," he says. In a recent survey of 1,200 employers, 78% said they valued kindness more than technical skills. However, only 15% of secondary schools included social and emotional skills in their curriculum. |
