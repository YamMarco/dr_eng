| TEACHER OVERVIEW — L18

LESSON 18  —  Time Management — Mark → Move → Return
P15: Mark hard question → Move immediately → Return at the end
40 min reading · 20 min writing — the target split

Objective
By the end of this lesson the student uses P15 in timed conditions and can allocate exam time correctly across all question types.
Key insight
"Most Module C students who fail do not fail because they do not know the content. They fail because they spend 12 minutes on one hard question and run out of time for the writing task. P15 is not optional — it is the single most practical pattern in the course."
Timing plan

Common mistakes to watch for
· Student "tries one more minute" on a hard question — P15 must be automatic, not negotiated
· Student spends 25+ minutes on reading and has only 5 minutes for writing
· Student returns to marked questions but now has no time — P15 requires noting the time before returning
· Student forgets to write their traffic light colour — reinforce: P8 + P15 work together |

| THE HOMEWORK QUESTION
I     Most students receive homework every day. Some experts argue that homework is essential for learning, while others believe it causes more harm than good. The debate has become more serious in recent years, as studies show that students today spend almost twice as much time on homework as students did 20 years ago. However, research suggests that more homework does not always mean better results.
II     Dr. Chloe Martin, a professor of education at Lyon University in France, studied 5,000 secondary school students over a period of three years. She found that students who spent more than two hours a night on homework were no more successful in exams than those who spent only 45 minutes. "The key is not the amount of homework, but the quality," says Dr. Martin.
III     Not everyone agrees with Dr. Martin's findings. Professor Alex Nkosi of the University of Cape Town argues that homework builds important habits, such as planning and self-discipline. However, both researchers agree on one point: homework should never take more than 90 minutes per evening for secondary school students. |
