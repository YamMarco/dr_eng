| TEACHER OVERVIEW — L8

LESSON 8  —  Two Correct Answers — Put a √ by the TWO Correct Answers
P11: Find one → Keep looking → Verify both have evidence

Objective
By the end of this lesson the student systematically finds TWO correct answers — without stopping at the first one — and verifies both with text evidence.
Key insight
"The biggest mistake in "two correct answers" questions: students find one correct answer and stop. The question is asking for TWO. The student who stops at one will always lose half the marks. P11 is simple: find one, keep looking, only stop when you have two with evidence."
Timing plan

Common mistakes to watch for
· Student marks only ONE answer and moves on — remind: the question asks for TWO
· Student finds two but does not check the evidence for both
· Student marks THREE options thinking "more is better" — P11 says exactly two
· Student confuses this format with MC — the √ format never has "circle the number" |

| THE JOB REVOLUTION
I     The world of work is changing faster than ever before. According to a recent report, over 85 million jobs will disappear in the next ten years because of automation and artificial intelligence. However, the same report predicts that 97 million new jobs will also appear. The problem is that many workers are not prepared for these new kinds of jobs.
II     Emma Clarke, a researcher at the Digital Skills Institute in London, believes that the solution lies in education. "Most schools still teach students skills for the jobs of yesterday, not the jobs of tomorrow," she says. According to Clarke, the three most important skills for the future are critical thinking, digital literacy, and communication.
III     Some governments have already begun to act. In 2023, the government of Singapore introduced a new programme that gives every adult worker 500 dollars a year to spend on training courses. More than 300,000 workers have already taken advantage of this programme. Professor Lee Min, an economist at National University of Singapore, says the results are encouraging: workers who complete at least one course a year are 35% less likely to lose their jobs. |
| --- |
| TEACHER — I DO
I DO — Q1 (two-correct format):
Q1: "What do we learn from paragraph I about the job market?"
Read all 5 options. Scan paragraph I.  P11: find one, keep looking.
i) "85 million jobs will disappear" → paragraph I says this. POSSIBLE. ✓ Keep looking.
ii) "Workers are well prepared for new jobs" → paragraph I says "not prepared" → FALSE. ✗ Eliminate.
iii) "97 million new jobs are predicted" → paragraph I says this. POSSIBLE. ✓ SECOND answer found.
iv) "Automation will end within 5 years" → not in text. ✗ Eliminate.
v) "The report was published in 2020" → text says no specific year for the report. ✗ Eliminate.
Answer: i) and iii). Both have evidence. Verify both. Mark √. |
| --- |
| ANSWER KEY
Answer: i) ✓ and iii) ✓
i) Evidence: "over 85 million jobs will disappear in the next ten years."
iii) Evidence: "97 million new jobs will also appear."
ii) FALSE — text says "not prepared". iv) and v) not in text. |

| THE READING DEBATE
Studies show that most young people today read less than they did 20 years ago. Dr. Yael Cohen of Tel Aviv University examined reading habits among 6,000 teenagers across five countries. She found that teenagers who read for at least 30 minutes a day had better vocabulary and stronger empathy skills than those who did not. However, Dr. Cohen found that the format of reading did not matter — reading on a screen was as effective as reading a printed book. Professor Sam Lee of Oxford University argues that schools should make reading a daily activity in all subjects, not only in language classes. |
