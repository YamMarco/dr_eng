| TEACHER OVERVIEW — L11

LESSON 11  —  Main Idea — What Is This Paragraph About?
P12: Topic sentence → covers the WHOLE paragraph → not a detail

Objective
By the end of this lesson the student identifies the main idea of a paragraph by focusing on the topic sentence and distinguishing it from supporting details.
Key insight
"The main idea covers everything in the paragraph. A detail is one example or piece of evidence inside it. The most common wrong answer in a main idea question IS a true detail from the paragraph — but it is not the main idea. If an option only covers ONE part of the paragraph, it is a detail, not the main idea."
Timing plan

Common mistakes to watch for
· Student picks a detail that is mentioned early in the paragraph — "early" does not mean "main"
· Student picks a very general option — too broad to be a main idea either
· Student skips the topic sentence and reads the middle of the paragraph
· Student cannot distinguish between a topic sentence and a supporting example |

| THE LAST FORESTS
I     Every year, the world loses approximately 15 billion trees. This destruction is happening faster than ever before, and scientists are deeply worried. Forests cover less than 30% of the earth's surface today, compared to 50% just 100 years ago. "The situation is critical," says Dr. Amara Diallo, director of the Global Forest Watch organisation. "Once they are gone, they cannot easily be replaced."
II     The causes of deforestation are well known. In most parts of the world, forests are cut down to make space for farming. This is particularly true in South America, where large areas of rainforest are destroyed every year to create farmland. Climate change makes the problem worse by causing longer and more severe droughts, which weaken trees and make them more likely to burn in forest fires.
III     However, there are reasons to be hopeful. Several countries have recently introduced strict laws to protect their forests. Professor Lena Berg of Stockholm University reports that in Scandinavia, the number of trees has actually increased by 40% over the past 50 years. "It is possible to stop deforestation," she says, "but it requires both governments and individuals to make difficult choices." |
| --- |
| TEACHER — I DO
I DO — Q1 (main idea of paragraph I):
Q1: "What is the main idea of paragraph I?"
Read paragraph I first sentence: "Every year, the world loses approximately 15 billion trees."
Does this cover the WHOLE paragraph? Yes — everything else in paragraph I supports this.
Options i) "The number of trees is falling" → this is what the main idea says. ✓
Options ii) "Dr. Diallo works for Global Forest Watch" → detail. Only covers her role.
Options iii) "Forests covered 50% of earth 100 years ago" → detail. One number only.
Answer: i) — the worldwide loss of trees is a serious and growing problem. |
| --- |
| 1.  What is the main idea of paragraph I?
Circle the correct answer. |
| --- |
| ANSWER KEY
Answer: i) — The worldwide loss of trees is a serious and growing problem.
Eliminated: ii) detail (Dr. Diallo). iii) detail (one number). iv) detail (one consequence). |

| THE PLASTIC PROBLEM
Scientists estimate that 8 million tonnes of plastic enter the world's oceans every year. This plastic comes from many sources, including industrial waste, household rubbish, and single-use packaging. Most of this plastic does not biodegrade — it simply breaks into smaller and smaller pieces called microplastics. Dr. Rania Khalil of the Mediterranean Environmental Institute warns that microplastics have been found in fish, drinking water, and even human blood. "The full effects on human health are still unknown," she says, "but we cannot afford to wait for more evidence before taking action." |
| --- |
| T1.  What is the main idea of the text?
Circle the correct answer. |
| --- |
| T2.  According to Dr. Rania Khalil, what does she warn about microplastics?
Give ONE answer. |
| --- |
| ANSWER KEY
T1: ii) — Plastic pollution in the oceans is a serious and growing problem that requires urgent action.
Covers all parts: 8 million tonnes + sources + microplastics + health effects + need for action.
T2: microplastics have been found in fish, drinking water, and even human blood.
P3: Dr. Khalil → her warning. |
