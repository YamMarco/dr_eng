| TEACHER OVERVIEW — L12

LESSON 12  —  Author's Attitude — How Does the Writer Feel?
Look for evaluative words → Prove with text language → Not your impression

Objective
By the end of this lesson the student identifies the author's attitude from evaluative adjectives and adverbs and proves it with exact text language.
Key insight
"A student's job is not to say how THEY feel about the topic — it is to identify how the WRITER feels. The clue is always in the word choices: 'unfortunately', 'surprisingly', 'clearly', 'it is alarming'. These are attitude words. They are Eye Catchers for this type of question."
Timing plan

Common mistakes to watch for
· Student writes their own opinion about the topic instead of the author's
· Student cannot find attitude words because they are not highlighted in the text
· Student confuses "neutral" with "balanced" — neutral means no clear attitude either way
· Student picks "optimistic" when the text is "cautiously hopeful" — too strong |

| THE LAST FORESTS
I     Every year, the world loses approximately 15 billion trees. This destruction is happening faster than ever before, and scientists are deeply worried. Forests cover less than 30% of the earth's surface today, compared to 50% just 100 years ago. "The situation is critical," says Dr. Amara Diallo, director of the Global Forest Watch organisation. "Once they are gone, they cannot easily be replaced."
II     The causes of deforestation are well known. In most parts of the world, forests are cut down to make space for farming. This is particularly true in South America, where large areas of rainforest are destroyed every year to create farmland. Climate change makes the problem worse by causing longer and more severe droughts, which weaken trees and make them more likely to burn in forest fires.
III     However, there are reasons to be hopeful. Several countries have recently introduced strict laws to protect their forests. Professor Lena Berg of Stockholm University reports that in Scandinavia, the number of trees has actually increased by 40% over the past 50 years. "It is possible to stop deforestation," she says, "but it requires both governments and individuals to make difficult choices." |

| TEACHER — I DO
I DO — Q1 (author's attitude, paragraph I):
Q1: "What is the author's attitude in paragraph I?"
Scan paragraph I for attitude words: "deeply worried" · "cannot easily be replaced" · "critical".
"deeply worried" → concerned. "critical situation" → alarm.
Answer: The author is concerned / alarmed about the loss of forests.
Prove with text: "scientists are deeply worried" + "the situation is critical." |

| THE CLEAN ENERGY TRANSITION
The world is moving towards clean energy faster than most experts predicted. Fortunately, the cost of solar power has fallen by more than 80% over the past ten years, making it cheaper than coal in most countries. However, scientists warn that the transition is not happening fast enough. "We need to triple the speed of the transition if we are to meet climate targets," says Dr. Sofia Reyes of the International Energy Agency. "The technology exists. The only question is whether governments have the political will to use it." |
| --- |
| T1.  What is the author's attitude in the text?
Circle the correct answer. |
| --- |
| T2.  What is the main idea of the text?
Circle the correct answer. |
| --- |
| ANSWER KEY
T1: iii) — Cautiously hopeful but concerned.
"Fortunately" = hopeful. "not happening fast enough" + "need to triple speed" = concerned.
T2: iii) — Clean energy is growing but not fast enough to meet climate targets.
Covers all parts: cost falling + warning about speed + need to triple. |
