| TEACHER OVERVIEW — L3

LESSON 3  —  Eye Catchers 2: Negative words, Key limiters, Contrast
P4: NOT/NEVER/NO → STOP — you are hunting for the FALSE statement
P5: main/most/only → narrows to ONE answer only
P6: however/but/although → shift → what comes AFTER is the new important information
Objective
By the end of this lesson the student automatically stops when they see a negative word, a key limiter, or a contrast word — and changes their reading strategy accordingly.
Key insight to convey
"NOT changes everything. Most students miss it and lose the answer. But the student who sees NOT first thinks: 'I am not looking for what IS true — I am looking for what is NOT true.' That is a completely different search."
Timing plan

Common mistakes to watch for
· Student reads "not all" as "not" — these are different: "not" = nothing, "not all" = some but not all
· Student sees "most effective" (P5) but picks an option about all types — P5 means ONLY the narrowest answer
· Student ignores "however" — they read the sentence before it and stop; the important info is AFTER "however"
· For P4 (NOT question): student answers with what IS true — the most common L3 error. Fix: say "You are hunting for FALSE." |

| GIVING TIME, GAINING HAPPINESS
I     Every year, millions of young people around the world choose to volunteer — to give their time to help others without payment. A study from a research institute found that young people who volunteer for at least two hours a week are 60% more likely to describe themselves as happy. Researchers were surprised. They had expected that money and success — not helping others — would be the main reason for happiness among teenagers.
II     The benefits of volunteering go beyond simple happiness. Dr. Sarah Okafor, a researcher at a European university, studied the effects of volunteering on mental health for ten years. She found that teenagers who volunteer feel less stressed and sleep better than those who do not. However, not all types of volunteering produce the same results. According to Okafor, the most effective programmes bring young people face to face with the people they help — not online activities.
III     Results from 12 countries show that the benefits of volunteering are not limited to the volunteers themselves. Schools that introduced volunteering programmes found that students became more responsible and more focused in class. Professor David Mills of an Australian university argues that helping others teaches young people skills that no classroom can ever replace. "When you help someone," he says, "you also help yourself." |
| --- |
| TEACHER
After reading — ask students to count:
Negative words: without, not, not, However, not all, not, not, no → 8
Key limiters: main, most → 2
Contrast word: However → 1
Ask: "If a question has the word 'not' in it — which pattern do you use?" → P4. Do not move on until this is clear. |

| P4 = NOT in question → STOP → I am hunting for the STATEMENT THAT IS FALSE |
| --- |
| TEACHER — I DO SCRIPT
SAY ALOUD — I DO for Q1 (P4):
Q1: "Which statement is NOT true according to paragraph II?"
STEP 1 — I read the question: "Which statement is NOT true?"
I see NOT. I say: STOP. P4 activates.
STEP 2 — P4: "I am NOT looking for what is true. I am hunting for the FALSE statement."
I read paragraph II carefully. I check each option against the text.
STEP 3 — CHECK EACH OPTION:
i) "Dr. Okafor studied mental health for ten years." → text says "ten years" ✓ TRUE. Not the answer.
ii) "All types of volunteering produce the same results." → text says "not all types produce the same results" → this IS false! ✓
iii) "Online activities are the most effective." → text says "not online" is the most effective → also false. BUT: ii) is more directly stated.
STEP 4 — ANSWER: ii) "All types of volunteering produce the same results" is NOT true.

Ask students: "What would have happened if they missed the word NOT?" → They would have chosen a TRUE statement. That is P4 in action. |

| THE SLEEP QUESTION
Most people know that sleep is important for health. However, a new study from the National Sleep Research Centre shows that the quality of sleep matters more than the quantity. Researchers found that people who sleep for six hours of deep, uninterrupted sleep feel more rested than those who sleep for nine hours but wake up several times during the night. Dr. Claire Webb, who led the study, says: "The most important factor is not the number of hours, but the depth of the sleep." She recommends that people avoid screens for at least one hour before going to bed. |
| --- |
| T1.  Which statement is NOT true according to the text?
Circle the number of the correct answer. |
| --- |
| T2.  What does the text say about people who sleep for nine hours?
Complete the answer. |
| --- |
| ANSWER KEY
T1: iii) — The number of hours you sleep is the most important factor.
P4: NOT true → text says "most important factor is NOT the number of hours" → iii) says it IS the number → false.
T2: ...may not feel as rested as those who sleep for six hours of uninterrupted sleep. (accept paraphrase)
P6: However → quality matters more than quantity → nine hours broken = less restful. |
