| TEACHER OVERVIEW — L1

LESSON 1  —  Strategic Reading: Not Word by Word
P1: Question → Keywords → Locate → Read → Answer
Objective
By the end of this lesson the student uses P1 to answer any factual question — without reading the whole text first.
Materials
Lesson sheet (this document, student pages only) · Colored pens or pencils for highlighting Eye Catchers · Whiteboard or projector to model P1
Timing plan

Common mistakes to watch for
· Student reads the whole text before looking at questions — redirect immediately to P1
· Student picks a keyword from the wrong part of the question ("what" instead of the topic keyword)
· Student finds the right paragraph but reads too much — remind: "read AROUND the keyword only"
· Student finishes YOU DO quickly and sits — have Challenge version ready |

| TEACHER
How to teach this vocabulary:
Step 1: Show the English word. Students try to guess the Hebrew (30 sec). Write on board.
Step 2: Reveal the Hebrew. Students say it aloud together.
Step 3: Show "In a question" example. Ask: "Where in a question would you see this word?"
Step 4: Students copy the Hebrew — no looking — just from memory.
Step 5 (fast check): Cover the Hebrew column. Teacher says English — student says Hebrew. |

| Word | Hebrew | In a question | In your mind: traffic light check |

| paragraph | פסקה | Read paragraph II and answer. | Which paragraph? → go there first. |

Quick check (end of vocab phase — 1 min):
Teacher says: "paragraph" → students say: "פסקה"
Teacher says: "according to" → students say: "לפי"
Teacher says: "explain" → students say: "מסביר"
If hesitation → repeat. These 5 words must be automatic by end of lesson. |

| 15–25 min | I DO — Teacher models P1 |
| --- | --- |
| TEACHER — PRE-TEXT
Before handing out the text:
Ask: "מה אתה בדרך כלל עושה כשאתה רואה טקסט? מי מתחיל לקרוא מהשורה הראשונה?"
Wait for hands. Then say: "היום אנחנו לומדים למה זה לא עובד — ומה לעשות במקום."
Hand out the text. Give students 30 seconds to LOOK at it (not read). Then cover it.
Ask: "What did your eyes jump to?" → Expected answers: numbers (30%, once, three times, two months), names (Dr. Klein), negatives (cannot, never, not). Say: "You already started using Eye Catchers." |

| LEARNING TO SWIM AS AN ADULT
I     Learning to swim as an adult is more common than many people think. According to a recent survey, over 30% of adults in large cities cannot swim. Many of them feel embarrassed about this and never try to learn. However, experts say it is never too late.
II     One study found that adults who learned to swim later in life often became stronger swimmers than those who learned as children. "Adults understand what the teacher explains and can correct their mistakes more quickly," says Dr. Anna Klein, a sports psychologist at the University of Oslo. "They are also not afraid to ask questions."
III     Adult swimming classes are now available in most cities. Some programs meet only once a week, while others offer three sessions a week. According to instructors, adults who practice regularly usually make good progress in less than two months. |
| --- |
| TEACHER — AFTER READING
Eye Catcher key (point to these while students look at text):
Numbers (amber): 30% · once a week · three sessions · two months
Names (purple): Dr. Anna Klein · University of Oslo
Negative words (red): cannot · never · never · not
Key words (green): most · only
Ask: "Which paragraph mentions Dr. Klein?" → II. "Where would you go for a question about her?" → II. |
| --- |
| P1  =  Question → Keywords → Locate → Read → Answer |
| --- |
| TEACHER — I DO SCRIPT
SAY ALOUD — exactly these words (students watch, no writing):
STEP 1 — QUESTION:  "What do we learn from paragraph I about adults who cannot swim?"
I am NOT reading the text yet.
STEP 2 — KEYWORDS:  "I see two keywords: 'paragraph I' and 'adults who cannot swim'."
Point to the question on the board. Circle "paragraph I" and "cannot swim".
STEP 3 — LOCATE:  "I go to paragraph I. I scan for the words 'cannot swim'."
Point to paragraph I in the text. Run finger along line 1.
STEP 4 — READ AROUND:  "I found it: 'cannot swim'. Now I read the sentence around it: 'Many of them feel embarrassed about this and never try to learn.'"
Underline that sentence on the board copy.
STEP 5 — ANSWER:  "Now I look at the options. Which one matches 'feel embarrassed → never try to learn'?"
Point to option iii. Read it: "What they usually do because they feel embarrassed." → That matches. Answer: iii.

After modelling, ask: "Could I have answered that without reading the whole text?" → YES.
Ask: "Which step was the most important?" → Many answers acceptable. Reinforce: Step 2 (keywords) and Step 3 (locate). |

| 25–35 min | WE DO — P1 together |
| --- | --- |
| TEACHER — WE DO GUIDE
WE DO — Teacher asks, students answer. Teacher writes on board.
Say: "Now we solve Q2 together. I will not give you the answer — you will find it."
Ask: "Step 2: What are the keywords in Q2?" → Expected: "Dr. Anna Klein" + "why"
Ask: "Step 3: Where do we go?" → Expected: paragraph II (name = paragraph pointer)
Ask: "Step 4: Read the sentence. What does Dr. Klein say?" → Students find and read aloud.
Ask: "Step 5: What is ONE reason? Write it." → Students write.
If students struggle with "give ONE answer": say "Pick ONE sentence from the text that answers WHY." |

| 2.  According to Dr. Anna Klein, why do adults become stronger swimmers than children?
Give ONE answer from paragraph II. |
| --- |
| ANSWER KEY
Accept ONE of these answers:
· Because adults understand what the teacher explains and can correct their mistakes more quickly.
· Because they are not afraid to ask questions.
Do NOT accept: "because they are stronger" (not from text) or "because they practice more" (not stated). |
| --- |
| TEACHER
After WE DO:
Ask: "How many sentences did you read to find that answer?" → Expected: 1–2.
Say: "That is P1. You did NOT read the whole paragraph. You located the keyword and read around it." |

| 35–55 min | YOU DO — P1 independently |
| --- | --- |
| TEACHER — YOU DO GUIDE
YOU DO — students work alone. Teacher circulates.
Say: "Now you do it alone. Use P1 for both questions. Write the answer in English from the text."
SUPPORT (students who need help): Write the keywords of Q3 on their sheet: "paragraph III + regularly".
STANDARD: no help — let them apply P1 without guidance.
CHALLENGE: after finishing, ask: "Find ONE distractor in Q4 that is almost right but wrong. Why is it wrong?"
Do NOT give answers while students are working. If stuck: "Re-read the question. Step 2: what are the keywords?" |

Point: "The distractor puts TRUE information in a FALSE answer. That is how Module C traps students." |

| 55–65 min | Timed Challenge — P1 + P15 under pressure |
| --- | --- |
| TEACHER
Setup (1 min):
Say: "8 minutes. One short text. Two questions. Use P1 for both."
Say: "If Q1 feels hard → P15: Mark it. Move to Q2. Come back."
Set a visible timer. Call time strictly at 8 minutes. No extensions.
After 8 min: students swap papers. Teacher reads answers. Students mark.
Ask: "Who used P15? Did it help?" → Normalize using Mark → Move → Return. |

| LEARNING A LANGUAGE ONLINE
Over the past ten years, online language learning has become very popular. According to a recent study, more than 500 million people worldwide now use language apps on their phones. Most of these learners are adults who want to improve their language skills for work or travel. However, not all apps are equally effective. Language experts say that apps work best when students also practice speaking with real people. "No app can replace a real conversation," says Dr. Yuki Tanaka, a language researcher at Tokyo University. |
