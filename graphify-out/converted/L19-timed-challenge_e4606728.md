| TEACHER OVERVIEW — L19

LESSON 19  —  Timed Challenge — Speed + Accuracy Under Exam Conditions
All patterns · 35 min reading · 20 min writing · P15 throughout

Objective
By the end of this lesson the student completes a full reading + writing set within strict time limits using all patterns automatically.
Key insight
"This lesson is the final preparation before the writing units. It is full exam conditions — not homework conditions. Students must finish. Every minute counts."
Timing plan

Common mistakes to watch for
· Student finishes reading in 20 min but writing takes 25 → must practice splitting time
· Student writes 50 words and thinks that is enough — count and rewrite
· Student uses no patterns and just reads through → return to pattern checklist
· Student's writing has no connector — connector habit is not yet automatic |

| THE DEEP SEA MYSTERY
I     More than 80% of our oceans remain unexplored. Scientists believe that the deep sea contains millions of species that humans have never seen. Each year, researchers discover approximately 2,000 new marine species — but many experts believe this is only a small fraction of what exists. "The deep ocean is the least understood environment on our planet," says Dr. Nina Kowalski, director of the Ocean Research Institute in Hamburg.
II     Exploring the deep sea is extremely difficult. The pressure at a depth of just one kilometre is 100 times greater than at the surface. In 2022, a team from the University of Tokyo sent a new type of robot to a depth of 8,000 metres. The robot discovered three new species of fish and collected rock samples that are approximately 500 million years old. "Every time we go deeper, we find something that surprises us," says Professor Kenji Yamamoto, who led the expedition.
III     Some of the most important medical discoveries of the past 30 years have come from deep-sea research. Several powerful medicines have been developed from chemicals found in sea creatures that live at depths of more than 2,000 metres. "We are destroying habitats that we have not even discovered yet," warns Dr. Kowalski. Scientists argue that protecting the deep sea is not only important for the environment, but also for human health. |
