- GIVING TIME, GAINING HAPPINESS
I     Every year, millions of young people around the world choose to volunteer — to give their time to help others without payment. A study from a research institute found that young people who volunteer for at least two hours a week are 60% more likely to describe themselves as happy. Researchers were surprised. They had expected that money and success — not helping others — would be the main reason for happiness among teenagers.
II     The benefits of volunteering go beyond simple happiness. Dr. Sarah Okafor, a researcher at a European university, studied the effects of volunteering on mental health for ten years. She found that teenagers who volunteer feel less stressed and sleep better than those who do not. However, not all types of volunteering produce the same results. According to Okafor, the most effective programmes bring young people face to face with the people they help — not online activities.
III     Results from 12 countries show that the benefits of volunteering are not limited to the volunteers themselves. Schools that introduced volunteering programmes found that students became more responsible and more focused in class. Professor David Mills of an Australian university argues that helping others teaches young people skills that no classroom can ever replace. "When you help someone," he says, "you also help yourself."
- 1.  Which statement is NOT true according to paragraph II?
Circle the number of the correct answer.
i)
Dr. Okafor studied volunteering and mental health for ten years.
ii)
All types of volunteering produce the same results.
iii)
Teenagers who volunteer feel less stressed than those who do not.
iv)
Dr. Okafor found that young volunteers sleep better.
Answer: ii) — All types of volunteering produce the same results.
P4 applied: NOT in question → hunt for false → text says "not all types of volunteering produce the same results."
Distractor: iii) is also false information about the text, but ii) directly contradicts "not all types are the same."
