MODULE G (016582, 5-point track, the highest level). One text of 450-500 words.

What the Module G answer keys show:

- Answers are the idea, not the detail: short, general phrases such as "stay ahead of competitors", "improve relations with the local community", "feel valued". The student must understand the point the question is about, not just find a nearby word.
- Questions often ask WHY, for what purpose, or what the writer explains. The answer must give that reason or idea. A detail that is true but does not answer the "why" / "how" is part of the points at most.
- The key often lists many accepted ideas (up to five, with OR): any one that the text supports is full points, in the student's own words or the text's.
- A short abstract answer is complete; a long answer that circles the idea without stating it is not.

Accepted answers in a real Module G answer key look like this:

- [Because the older employees needed to] learn digital skills / learn (how) to use the internet / learn more about using the internet OR adopt (a/the) new technology.
- [He explains how companies can] stay ahead of competitors OR (have a workforce that can) quickly adopt new (AI) technologies OR help older workers/employees (continuously) update their AI skills.
- [The marketer needs to be able to] identify the most suitable influencer to advertise a particular product.
